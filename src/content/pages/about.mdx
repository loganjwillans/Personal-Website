## What I actually do

Most of my work is enterprise software, which means the interesting constraints are never on a
moodboard. Legal teams are measured on risk and cycle time; sales teams are measured on speed. Both
use the same tool, and neither has time to learn it. Designing there means spending more time on
permissions, states and exceptions than on colour.

<Card title="The surface I own" color="blue">

At IntelAgree I own design for the contract workspace: the place where a redlined document, a clause
library and a review checklist all have to agree with each other. It is less like designing a screen
and more like designing a set of rules that a screen can follow.

</Card>

## How I work

<LooseCard title="Four habits" color="cream">

- **Start with the artefact.** A real contract, a real review, a real support ticket. Abstract
  personas are a last resort.
- **Prototype to disagree.** A clickable prototype settles an argument faster than a slide, and it
  makes the edge cases visible early.
- **Design the system, not the screen.** Components, states and content rules outlive any single
  feature.
- **Write it down.** Decisions with their trade-offs, in the repo, where engineers can find them.

</LooseCard>

<Callout label="A useful constraint" title="Time is the real material" tone="blue">

Nobody in this workflow has time to learn a tool. Every extra decision I ask a reviewer to make is a
cost, whether or not it shows up in a usability test.

</Callout>
