Intake was the step before any real work happened: someone in sales needed a contract, and had to
describe what kind it was, to whom, for how long, and under which paper. The form had forty fields,
eleven of them mandatory, and a help article nobody opened.

This case study is protected because it includes internal process detail. The password opens the
full write-up: the research approach, the two competing models we prototyped, what we shipped, and
the parts that did not land.

<Card title="The short version" label="Outcome" color="blue">

We replaced the single form with a guided intake that asks three questions, then reveals only what
the chosen path needs. Median completion time dropped by more than half, and "wrong template"
support tickets fell by two thirds.

</Card>

## The two models

<Callout
  label="Decision"
  title="Progressive disclosure beat a smarter form"
  color="green"
  foot="Trade-off: power users who request the same contract type weekly lost their single-page shortcut."
>
  We tested a smarter single form with conditional logic against a multi-step flow that branched
  early. The single form was faster for the 5% of users who knew exactly what they wanted. The
  branching flow was dramatically better for everyone else — and everyone else was 80% of volume.
</Callout>

<LightboxImage
  src="/images/work/contract-intake.svg"
  alt="Three-step intake flow branching by contract type, with a summary step before submission."
  caption="Click to zoom: the branch as we shipped it, with the summary step before submission."
/>

<LooseCard title="The part that did not land" color="cream">

We tried to preserve a "repeat last request" shortcut for frequent requesters. It tested well with
people who already knew the form, but sat awkwardly inside a flow designed for people who did not.
We cut it rather than complicate the main path, and gave frequent users a saved template instead.

</LooseCard>

<Quote>

Nobody reads instructions at the moment they are most stressed. Design for that moment, not for the
calm one in which the help article was written.

</Quote>

<Callout label="Trade-off" title="A shortcut for some, a clearer path for most" tone="yellow" foot="We would make the same call again.">

The 5% who lost their one-page form were the loudest voices in testing. The other 80% never had to
write a support ticket about picking the wrong template again.

</Callout>
