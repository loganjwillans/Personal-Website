The corner had everything a reading corner is supposed to have: a chair, a lamp, a window. I kept
moving all the books to the kitchen table, because the corner quietly required me to tidy up before
I could use it.

<LightboxImage
  src="/projects/reading-desk/poster.svg"
  alt="A stack of paper and a cup on a desk"
  caption="The rebuilt corner, such as it is. Click the image to zoom."
/>

<Card title="What changed" label="Before and after" color="yellow">

- The lamp moved from behind the chair to one side, so it lit the page rather than the wall.
- The loose paper got one tray with a hard limit. When the tray is full, something has to leave.
- The notebook now stays open on the desk. Putting it away was the real reason I never started.

</Card>

The useful lesson had nothing to do with furniture. I had designed the corner for a version of
myself who tidies first and reads second. The rebuild designed for the version who arrives with a
coffee and sits down immediately.

<LooseCard title="What I would keep" color="cream">

One rule survived: a surface should be usable in the state you find it, not only in the state you
left it. Everything else — the tray, the lamp position, the chair — is incidental.

</LooseCard>

<Quote>

A desk is a piece of software you have to inhabit.

</Quote>
